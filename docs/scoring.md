# How scoring works

Short version: your result is a scaled score from 100 to 1,000, and you pass at 720. The score report also shows your percent correct in each domain, but only the total scaled score decides pass or fail.

## Scaled score, not percent correct

Anthropic reports results on a scaled range of 100 to 1,000, with a cut score of 720 for all four certifications. The official FAQ explains why: scaled scoring equates scores across exam forms that may differ slightly in difficulty. Two candidates who sit different forms are held to the same standard.

What this means for you: **do not convert 720 into a percentage of items.** Anthropic does not publish how many correct answers a 720 needs, and the number can differ between forms. Treat any "you need X out of 60" claim online as a guess.

## Unscored items

From December 7, 2026, the Associate, Foundations; Developer, Foundations; and Architect, Professional exams have 70 items, of which 60 are scored and 10 are unscored. The exam guides say unscored items:

- are being tested for use on future forms
- are not identified, and are mixed in with the scored items
- do not count toward your score

So treat every item as if it counts. You cannot tell which ten are experimental, and trying to guess wastes time.

Architect, Foundations has 60 items. Its guide does not mention unscored items.

## What the score report shows

| Part | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Pass or fail | The result. Based only on your total scaled score. |
| Scaled score, 100 to 1,000 | Where you landed against the 720 cut |
| Percent correct by domain | Where you were strong and weak. Useful for planning a retake. Not used to decide pass or fail. |

You see your score on screen at the end of the exam. Test center candidates also get a printed report. If you pass, Credly emails you to claim your badge, usually within minutes for online exams.

## What a good score means

A pass is a pass: the credential does not show a grade beyond the badge. A strong practice target, as our study guidance and not an official figure, is to score 80% or better on well-made practice items in every domain before you book. That leaves room for harder forms, unfamiliar scenarios and nerves.

Watch the domain breakdown more than the total. A big gap in a heavy domain can sink a result even if everything else is strong.

## Retakes

From the official exam guides and [Pearson VUE's Anthropic page](https://www.pearsonvue.com/us/en/anthropic.html):

- you can retake after a waiting period: 14 days after the first attempt, 30 days after the second, 90 days after the third
- you can take each exam up to four times in any rolling 12-month period
- limits are per exam, so failing one exam does not stop you registering for another
- the exam fee applies to each attempt

## Validity and renewal

A certification is valid for 12 months from the date it is awarded. Anthropic's FAQ says renewal involves reviewing what has changed and completing a free, non-proctored assessment on the Anthropic Partner Academy. If your certification lapses, you retake the full exam to regain certified status.
