# Study plans

Two plans that work for any of the four exams: one for a week, one for when the exam is two days away. Both assume you have done the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md) and know your two or three weakest domains. Expect 1 to 2 hours a day on the 7-day plan.

These exams test judgment in realistic work scenarios. Reading helps, but the fastest gains come from doing the work: building, configuring or reviewing real Claude output while you study.

## 7-day plan

| Day | Focus | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Map the exam | Read your exam's overview page and the official exam guide, including its sample questions. Do the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md). Rank your domains by (weight x gap). |
| 2 | Heaviest weak domain | Read its page in full. Do all 10 items. For each miss, write the trap you fell for. Then spend 30 minutes doing that skill for real in Claude, Claude Code or the API. |
| 3 | Second weak domain | Same routine as day 2. |
| 4 | Third weak domain, plus a review | Same routine for the third. Then reread your miss notes from days 2 and 3. |
| 5 | Timed practice | Take a timed practice run, such as the free PrepClubs diagnostic for your exam (links on the [diagnostic page](diagnostic.md)). Score it by domain. |
| 6 | Close the remaining gaps | Revisit any domain under 80% on day 5. Read the "Key ideas to know cold" tables for every domain. |
| 7 | Light review and logistics | Reread the traps lists only. Run Pearson's system test if you test online. Read the [exam-day checklist](exam-day-checklist.md). Stop by early evening. |

### How to review a missed item

1. **Name the deciding constraint you missed.** Most items turn on one fact in the stem: a data rule, a scale figure, an audience, a failure mode.
2. **Name the trap.** For example: picked the most powerful option instead of the simplest that works, trusted output without checking, added a human step where a technical control was needed.
3. **Write the rule you will use next time** in one line.

Keep these in one list. The same few traps repeat across domains.

## 2-day plan

For when the exam is booked and close.

### Day 1

| Block | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 30 to 45 min | Do the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md). |
| 2 | 20 min | Read the "Traps that cost points" section of every domain page for your exam. |
| 3 | 90 min | For your two weakest heavy domains, read the full page and do all 10 items. |
| 4 | 45 min | Take a timed practice run and review every miss. |

### Day 2 (exam day or the day before)

| Block | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 30 min | Redo yesterday's misses without looking at the answers. |
| 2 | 20 min | Read the "Key ideas to know cold" tables for all domains. |
| 3 | 10 min | Read the [exam-day checklist](exam-day-checklist.md). If testing online, run Pearson's system test now. |

## Exam-specific notes

- **Associate, Foundations:** practise in Claude itself. Build a Project with instructions and knowledge, try connectors and Skills, and deliberately check outputs for invented specifics. Output evaluation is the heaviest domain.
- **Developer, Foundations:** write code. Build a small tool-using agent with the API, handle errors and retries, add a structured output with validation, and try prompt caching. Applications and Integration is the heaviest domain.
- **Architect, Foundations:** work through the six official scenarios in the exam guide and design each one end to end. Agentic architecture and orchestration is more than a quarter of the exam.
- **Architect, Professional:** practise defending decisions. For each domain, write the one constraint that would change your design (residency, evaluation gap, audit need) and what you would do. Integration is the heaviest domain.

## What not to do

- **Do not use exam dumps.** Recalled questions are often wrong and break the exam rules, and scenario-based items change between forms.
- **Do not memorise product trivia at the expense of judgment.** Items describe a situation and ask for the best decision.
- **Do not skip the official exam guide.** Anthropic's FAQ calls it the authoritative source for exam scope.
